# This Is Not a Drill — full script (draft 1)

Long-form horror, 16:9, **about 11 minutes**, ~2,130 spoken words. Dong narrates the whole way through, like *Two Legs*: no long silences, each chapter climbs, gives a small moment of relief, then drops harder. The few lines other people say are heard in their own voices.

**The thing in the hallway:** it is never shown clearly until the last shot. It can't open doors. It can only copy voices it has heard, and it needs someone inside to open the door for it. **Every line the thing says is captioned in red**, even when it sounds exactly like someone we know.

**Pacing rules for the voice-over and edit:**
- Gaps between lines are no longer than about 0.4 s. A sound effect (a knock, the handle, a phone buzz) plays *under* the narration, never in a gap. The only real silences are marked *(beat)*.
- Dong narrates quietly and fast, like he's still scared someone will hear him. He slows down only for the last four lines.
- Each chapter's last line runs straight into the next chapter's first. No fade to black until the very end.

**Voices (ElevenLabs, one take per line):**

**Wardrobe: Westbrook High uniform (every student, including Dong):**
- White short-sleeve polo shirt with navy collar trim and a small navy hawk crest on the left chest (it replaces the logo on Dong's tee).
- Navy trousers or a navy pleated skirt, and white sneakers.
- Dong keeps his black beanie and round glasses.
- The adults dress as themselves:
  - Ms. Harlow: cardigan and long skirt.
  - Principal Dawes: shirt, tie and lanyard.
  - Mom: casual clothes and a car coat.
  - The deputy: a sheriff's uniform.

**How the cast is built:** every character is a puppet on Dong's rig, using the same joints, sizes and clips. Walking, running, sitting, crouching, turning and the scared poses all carry over. Each new character is drawn in the same pose as Dong's hero image so their pieces drop straight onto his skeleton. The students are about Dong's height; the adults are slightly taller.

## Chapter 1: Code Red (≈1:45)

*Establishing shot: Westbrook High from the street, early morning. A red-brick two-storey school with a flagpole and a sign reading WESTBROOK HIGH SCHOOL · HOME OF THE HAWKS. Yellow buses unload kids in uniform. The camera drifts up to a second-floor window at the far end: Room 214.*
**DONG:** Every school in America has a lockdown drill. You learn it in kindergarten, the same way you learn your address. Lights off. Lock the door. Cover the window. Get in the corner away from the door. Phones on silent. And the most important rule of all: you don't open that door for anyone. Not a teacher, not a friend, not your own mom. When it's really over, someone with a key will open it for you.
*Title card THIS IS NOT A DRILL, over the narration.*
*Inside: the hallway, morning. Lockers, posters, a banner: GO HAWKS.*
**DONG:** I've done that drill maybe forty times. And on October twelfth, at ten fourteen in the morning, in Room 214, I found out why that last rule exists.
*Room 214. Ms. Harlow at the board, the class half asleep.*
**DONG:** Third period, English. Ms. Harlow was reading Poe out loud. Tyler was drawing on his arm. Priya, who sits next to me, was actually taking notes. And then the speaker over the door clicked on.
**PRINCIPAL DAWES** *(intercom)***:** Attention, all students and staff. Lockdown. Lockdown. This is not a drill. Lockdown.
**DONG:** No "good morning." No "Go Hawks." Principal Dawes says "Go Hawks" at the end of everything, even the fire alarm announcements. This time the speaker just clicked off.
*Fast cuts: lights off, the lock, black paper over the window, desks scraping.*
**DONG:** Ms. Harlow had the door locked before he finished. Lights off. Priya slapped the black paper over the window. Twenty-three of us crammed into the corner by the bookshelf, knees in each other's backs, and Ms. Harlow crouched in front of us, finger on her lips.
**MS. HARLOW** *(whisper)***:** Not a word. Not one. No matter what you hear.
**DONG:** I'd done this forty times, and it always felt like a game. Somebody always giggles. Somebody always whispers "what if it's real" in a spooky voice. This time nobody giggled. Ms. Harlow's hands were shaking so hard she had to hold one with the other. Tyler leaned over and whispered, "Bet it's a raccoon in the vents again," and Ms. Harlow looked at him in a way I'd never seen a teacher look at anyone, and he shut up.
**DONG:** We waited for sirens. For helicopters. For anything. And for six minutes, we heard nothing at all. Then, from the far end of the hallway, footsteps.

## Chapter 2: The Knock (≈1:50)

*The hallway, low and empty. The footsteps are heard, never seen.*
**DONG:** They weren't running. That's what scared me. In every drill they tell you what to listen for, and nobody ever told us about this. Slow. Even. Like somebody walking to their car on a Sunday. And there was something else, something I didn't understand until later. Every few steps, the footsteps changed. Sneakers squeaking on the floor. Then heels, clicking. Then heavy work boots. Like whoever it was couldn't decide who they wanted to be.
*A door handle rattles somewhere down the hall.*
**DONG:** Then the doors. Room 208, the handle rattled. Two tries, then nothing. Room 210. Rattle, rattle. Room 212, right next door. And then the footsteps stopped right outside ours.
*Our door from inside. The handle tilts down, slowly, and stops at the lock.*
**DONG:** The handle went down, slowly, all the way to the lock, and stayed there. Nobody breathed. Tyler had both hands over his mouth. And then three knocks. Soft. Polite.
**THE THING** *(Dawes' voice, red)***:** Ms. Harlow? It's Principal Dawes. It's okay. It's over. You can all come out now.
*Ms. Harlow, frozen. Then her shoulders drop with relief.*
**DONG:** It was him. Same voice, same tired, warm, dad voice that reads the lunch menu every morning. A couple of girls actually started crying, the good kind of crying. Ms. Harlow let out this breath like she'd been holding it for six minutes, and she stood up, smoothed her skirt like she always does before parent night, and started toward the door.
**PRIYA** *(whisper)***:** He has keys.
**DONG:** Priya grabbed her sleeve. "He has keys," she whispered. Principal Dawes has a master key to every room in the building. If it was really over, why was he knocking?
*The intercom clicks on. Breathing. Panicked.*
**PRINCIPAL DAWES** *(intercom, frantic)***:** Do not open the door. Do not open the door for anyone. Including me.
**DONG:** The intercom. Same voice, but breathless, terrified, coming from the speaker above our heads, from the office, all the way down on the first floor. And then the speaker clicked off, and from the other side of our door, in exactly the same voice, very slowly, someone repeated it.
**THE THING** *(Dawes' voice, red, slow)***:** Including. Me.
**DONG:** Then the footsteps walked away. Slow. Even. Like it had all the time in the world. Ms. Harlow sat back down in front of us, very carefully, like the floor might break. She didn't look at us. She just stared at the door, and I watched her lips moving, and I realized she was counting us. Over and over. Twenty-three. Twenty-three. Twenty-three.

## Chapter 3: The Group Chat (≈1:50)

*Cutaway: the school from outside, now silent. Every window dark, blinds down, nobody on the lawn. A crow on the sign.*
*Phones glowing under knees, screens dimmed.*
**DONG:** For an hour, nobody moved. Then the phones started. Everyone had them on silent, but you could see the glow, under knees and in sleeves, and the class group chat was going crazy.
*Phone: the chat. Messages pop in under the narration.*
**DONG:** "Is anyone else hearing knocking?" "It's in the science wing." "It said it was the nurse." And then one from Kayla in Room 208. "Coach Reyes is outside. He says it's clear. We're opening." *(beat)* And after that, nothing from Kayla. Nothing from anyone in 208. Ever.
**DONG:** Ms. Harlow saw the screens and, for once, she didn't take a single phone. She just pulled hers out too. And the messages kept coming. "Something knocked on 204 in Mr. Kim's voice. Mr. Kim is IN 204." "It knows our names." "Why is nobody coming?"
**DONG:** Then a message from someone in 210. "Don't talk. It's listening. It only knows voices it's heard." And I remembered that every time someone knocks, the voice on the other side is someone you know. Someone it's heard. It wasn't guessing. It was learning, and we'd been feeding it.
*Dong, hunched, phone pressed to his ear. Priya glaring.*
**DONG:** And this is the part I'd take back if I could. My mom had texted me twelve times. So I called her. I know. I put my hand over the phone and I whispered so quietly.
**DONG** *(whisper, on the phone)***:** Mom, I'm okay. We're in 214. I love you. I'll call you after.
**DONG:** She didn't say anything back. I could just hear her breathing, fast, the way she breathes when she's trying not to cry, and then she whispered "okay, baby," and I hung up before she could hear me crying too.
**DONG:** Priya looked at me like I'd just set off a firework. And out in the hallway, right outside our door, something that had been completely silent for an hour shifted its weight. It had been standing there the whole time. And now it had my voice. My name. My mom. And our room number.

## Chapter 4: Tyler (≈1:50)

*The door. The thin strip of light underneath it. Two shadows: feet, standing perfectly still.*
**DONG:** For the next hour, it went from door to door down the whole floor. We could hear it trying voices like someone trying on coats. Coach Reyes. The lunch lady. Somebody's little sister, asking for her brother by name. Every so often, one of the voices would get something slightly wrong, a word too slow, a laugh in the wrong place, and it would stop, and try again, and get it right.
**DONG:** At noon it came back to us. Three knocks, soft, polite. And then a voice we all knew, crying.
**THE THING** *(Tyler's voice, red, sobbing)***:** Guys? Guys, it's me, it's Tyler. I was in the bathroom when it started. Please open the door. It's right behind me, it's right behind me, please!
*Everyone turns. Tyler is in the corner with them, white, shaking his head.*
**DONG:** Twenty-two heads turned at the same time to look at Tyler. And Tyler was right there, in the corner, between Marcus and me, shaking his head so hard his teeth were rattling. He'd only said one thing all morning. One joke, when we first got in the corner. That was enough.
**THE THING** *(Tyler's voice, red)***:** Please. Please. Pl— *(stops)*
**DONG:** And then it stopped crying. Just like that, mid-word, like someone switching off a radio. And very softly, through the door, in Tyler's voice, it laughed.
*The black paper on the window. One corner peeling.*
**DONG:** Tyler started crying then, for real, the quiet kind, with his face pressed into his knees, and Marcus put a hand on his back. Nobody said it, but we were all thinking the same thing. Tyler had said one sentence. Just one. And now it owned his voice. I thought about everything I'd said that morning. Every answer in class. The phone call. Every word.
**DONG:** Then I saw the corner of the black paper on the window had peeled down, just a little, just a triangle of glass. And I know the rule. I know. But I had to know what it was.
*Dong's POV, creeping toward the gap. Through the glass: the hallway, empty. Lights flickering. Nothing.*
**DONG:** I crawled closer and looked through the gap. And the hallway was empty. Completely empty, all the way to the stairs. Nothing at our door. Nothing anywhere. *(beat)* But the shadow of two feet under our door didn't move. And then, from right there, right on the other side of the glass, where nothing was standing, it whispered my name.
**THE THING** *(Dong's voice, red, whisper)***:** Dong.

## Chapter 5: Clear (≈1:40)

*Late afternoon light through the blinds. Kids asleep sitting up.*
**DONG:** It didn't come back. We sat in that corner for five more hours. Nobody ate, nobody talked. Marcus fell asleep sitting up. At four forty, there were heavy footsteps, a lot of them, and then the one sound we'd been waiting for all day. A key, in the lock.
*The door opens. Flashlights. A deputy in the doorway.*
**DEPUTY:** Sheriff's department. Stay where you are. We're bringing you out.
**DONG:** They had keys. They didn't knock, and they didn't ask. They walked us out single file, hands on the shoulder of the person in front. And as we went down the hall, I looked into Room 208. The door was wide open. Every backpack was still on its desk. A water bottle was rolling slowly on the floor. Nobody was in there.
*Wide: the school exterior at dusk, washed red and blue by police lights. Second floor: five classroom doors' windows glow, open, with no one inside.*
*The front lawn. Police lights, buses, parents behind tape.*
**DONG:** On the stairs, I counted the open doors on our floor. Five. Five rooms that heard someone they trusted and did exactly what any normal person would do. Nobody from those rooms was outside with us. Nobody from those rooms was ever found.
**DONG:** Outside, it was chaos. Parents, police, news vans. I heard a deputy telling Ms. Harlow they'd checked every security camera in the building, and all day long, the second-floor hallway was empty. No one walking. No one knocking. Just doors, rattling, one by one, on their own.
*Mom pushes through the crowd and grabs Dong.*
**DONG:** They sat us all in the gym across the street, and a lady from the district with a clipboard asked every single one of us the same question. "Did anyone in your room open the door?" Not "are you okay." Not "what did you see." Just that. And every time someone said no, she let out this tiny breath and wrote something down, and I remember thinking, she knows. Whatever this is, she's seen it before.
**DONG:** And then my mom came through the crowd and grabbed me so hard she lifted me off the ground, and for the first time all day I let myself breathe. It was over. We'd done everything right. We never opened the door.

## Chapter 6: Lock Your Door (≈1:45)

*Night. Dong's house. His bedroom, lamp off, the door.*
**DONG:** That night, my mom made me grilled cheese, and sat on my bed until I was almost asleep, and on her way out she said something she has never said to me in seventeen years.
**MOM:** Lock your door tonight, honey.
**DONG:** So I did. And at three in the morning, I woke up to three knocks. Soft. Polite.
**THE THING** *(Mom's voice, red)***:** Dong? It's Mom. Can you open the door, sweetheart? I just want to check on you.
*His phone lights up on the nightstand. A text.*
**DONG:** It was perfect. Her voice. The way she says sweetheart, a little bit too long, like she's tired. I could even hear her keys jingling, the little rubber duck keychain I gave her in fifth grade. Every single part of me wanted to open that door, because it was my mom, and I'd had the worst day of my life, and I just wanted my mom.
**DONG:** I was halfway out of bed when my phone lit up. A text. From Mom. "Don't open your door. I'm in the car outside. Somebody called me an hour ago from your number. It sounded exactly like you. It said you needed me to go and get you from school."
*Close on Dong. The handle of his door begins to move.*
**DONG:** I read it three times. Then I looked at the bottom of my door, at the strip of hallway light, and there were two shadows in it. Two feet, standing perfectly still. The same way they stood outside Room 214.
**DONG:** It never broke into the school. It can't. It just needs one person to open one door. And it had my name, my voice, my mom's voice, and now it knew where I lived. Because I'd told it. Because I couldn't keep quiet for one day.
**THE THING** *(Dong's voice, red, through the door)***:** Dong. It's me. Open the door. *(beat)* Go Hawks.
*Dong, alone, back against the wall, facing the door.*
**DONG** *(slowing, quiet)***:** That was eleven days ago. I haven't opened my door since. My mom leaves food in the hallway, and I wait until I hear her car leave before I reach out. She texts me every night now, a code word, one we made up that I've never said out loud. Not once. Not even to myself. And some nights, after she's gone to bed, the knocking starts again, and it tries every voice it knows. Ms. Harlow. Tyler. Principal Dawes. My mom. Me. It's very patient. It has all the time in the world.
**DONG** *(slower)***:** So if your school ever goes into lockdown, don't whisper. Don't call anyone. Don't make a sound.
**DONG** *(slowest)***:** And if you ever hear my voice outside your door… it isn't me.
*Final shot: the house hallway, dark. Standing perfectly still outside Dong's bedroom door, Dong — with glowing yellow-green eyes, smiling.*

*Smash to black on the last word. End card.*
